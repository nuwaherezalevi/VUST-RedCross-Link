OUR PROGRAMMES

Practical action. Human impact.

Our Constitution identifies a broad range of humanitarian programmes. Activities are delivered subject to capacity, approvals, training and relevant partner guidance.

PROGRAMME AREAS

Where our service can make a difference.

01

Community health & health promotion

Health education, disease prevention, healthy lifestyles, sanitation, mental health awareness and public health campaigns.

Who it serves: Students, staff, schools and community members.

Potential benefits: Greater awareness, prevention knowledge and informed health choices.

02

First Aid & capacity building

First Aid training and services, Basic Life Support learning and related capacity-building opportunities through appropriately qualified and authorized providers.

Who it serves: Members, volunteers and participating community groups.

Potential benefits: Improved preparedness and practical skills. Training and certification are subject to provider requirements.

03

Voluntary blood donation

Support voluntary blood donation awareness and campaigns in coordination with relevant health partners.

Who it serves: Potential donors and communities served by blood services.

Potential benefits: Increased awareness and support for voluntary donation.

04

Disaster preparedness & response

Disaster risk reduction, preparedness awareness, response support, recovery and community resilience activities.

Who it serves: Students and communities exposed to risks and emergencies.

Potential benefits: Better risk awareness, readiness and coordinated community action.

05

Environmental conservation & climate action

Environmental conservation, tree planting, climate change adaptation and initiatives that support climate resilience and disaster risk reduction.

Who it serves: Campus and neighbouring communities.

Potential benefits: Stronger environmental awareness and local stewardship.

06

Water, sanitation & hygiene (WASH)

Community awareness and projects promoting safe water, sanitation and hygiene practices.

Who it serves: Schools, households and community groups.

Potential benefits: Improved knowledge of hygiene and sanitation practices.

07

Psychosocial support & safeguarding

Promote mental health awareness, psychosocial wellbeing, safe participation, dignity and protection from abuse.

Who it serves: Members, volunteers and communities, within appropriate competence and referral arrangements.

Potential benefits: Safer, more inclusive and supportive environments.

08

Youth leadership, outreach & innovation

Youth empowerment, volunteer development, school outreach, road safety awareness, humanitarian research, digital record management and innovation.

Who it serves: Students, young people, schools and partner organizations.

Potential benefits: Leadership development, collaboration and evidence-informed service.

Important: Programme descriptions explain areas identified in the Constitution; they do not confirm that every programme is currently active or that a service is available on demand. Contact the Link to verify current activities.
